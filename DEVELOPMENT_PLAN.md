# Development Plan & Coach Roadmap

## Outcome goals
- Short-term performance goal: score 10+ points in a league game.
- Stretch performance goal: score 20 points in a league game.
- Broader goal: become a well-rounded impactful player—scoring, playmaking, defense, decision-making and athletic development.
These are goals, not promises or time-bound predictions.

## Current phase — Evidence gathering + foundational skill improvement
Priority areas currently hypothesized from initial data:
1. Catch-and-shoot preparation and flow.
2. Balanced/repeatable pull-up shooting.
3. Finishing under pressure (angles, touch, ball protection, timing).
4. Progressive three-point range and consistency.
5. Ongoing evaluation of handle, playmaking, defense and athletic qualities.

## Tracking and review loop
- Log each reported individual session and meaningful club/SKS/game observations in TRAINING_LOG.
- Store comparable test results and skill-specific numbers in SKILL_METRICS.
- Store coaching interpretations, hypotheses, and decisions in COACH_NOTES.
- Keep the profile and current plan updated as evidence changes.
- Review priorities periodically, and after games when available.
- Record game stats/context where possible: minutes, points, field goals by type, FT, turnovers, assists, rebounds, defensive matchups/events, role, game result and observations. Do not invent missing stats.

## Athlete reporting
For individual sessions: exercises, attempt/make numbers, effort (rough RPE if comfortable), fatigue, what felt/failed and any pain.
For games: minutes and stats if known; describe possessions or situations rather than only final scoring; report role and opponent context.

## Updated phase — First official game evidence review (2026-11-25)
### Initial DALK baseline (2 games)
- 57:46 total minutes (28.9 MPG), 9 points (4.5 PPG), 3/14 2PT (21.4%), 0/1 3PT, 3/7 FT (42.9%), 7 rebounds (3.5 RPG), 0 AST, 1 STL, 9 turnovers (4.5 TOPG).
- User confirms PG role; most turnovers came from physical contact; missed 2PT shots were primarily drives/layups.
- Two games are too small a sample to establish stable performance levels. Use as a baseline and gather more detailed context.
### Revised priority order
1. **Drive finishing under pressure:** improve approach angle, controlled gather, takeoff balance, release touch and protected finishes. Track contested/uncontested attempts and help-defense presence.
2. **Ball security through legal contact:** body positioning, low stable base, protect ball on far side, strong gather, pivot/escape options. Avoid seeking collisions.
3. **PG reads after penetration:** identify rim defender/help; choose finish, kick-out, controlled stop/floater or reset.
4. **Press/trap handling:** scan early, maintain pivot options, outlet before trap closes, relocate after pass.
5. **Left-hand handle and finishing:** improve weak-side options without sacrificing control.
6. **Shooting mechanics and selection:** keep practice tests separate from official game stats; gradually improve shot quality and confidence.
7. **Rebounding, defense and playmaking:** continue development and collect more evidence; 0 official assists in two games does not by itself establish poor playmaking.
### Training design implications
- Include short, technically controlled drive-finishing blocks with progressive, safe defensive pressure rather than high-volume collisions.
- Include partner pad/contact only if supervised and controlled; no deliberate dangerous impact or repeated hard contact.
- Add ball-protection decisions: secure gather, pivot away from pressure, outlet pass, and reset.
- Keep workload age-appropriate and coordinate with club/SKS sessions. Do not add extra high-intensity work during match taper.
### Review criteria
- Reassess after at least 3–5 additional games where possible.
- Compare turnovers per game and per minute, drive conversion with contest context, FT attempts/conversion, and decision quality. Do not use plus/minus alone as an individual performance measure.
