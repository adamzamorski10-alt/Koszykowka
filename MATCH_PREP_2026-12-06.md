## Tactical preparation for the user's PG role

### 1. Bring the ball over half court vs full-court pressure
- Before receiving: scan both sides, identify the middle outlet and nearest safety valve.
- Create width: one teammate wide on each side, one middle flash, one deep outlet when team structure allows.
- Use pass-and-move, not stationary dribbling into a sideline trap.
- If pressured: protect with body, keep dribble alive, change pace/direction, retreat dribble only to create a new angle, then pass to the release player.
- Avoid risky cross-court passes across defenders and avoid picking up the dribble without a clear pass.
- If two defenders trap: first read is usually the open teammate/release pass, not trying to beat both defenders alone.
- Ask club coach what press-break alignment/terminology the team uses; team system takes precedence.

### 2. Create scoring chances without forcing
- Attack closeouts and gaps after the defense shifts; don't drive into a set wall.
- Use a change of pace and attack the defender's top foot/hip; keep the first dribble purposeful.
- If the first defender is beaten and a rim protector steps across: eurostep, stride stop, reverse or pass to the open teammate based on the actual help position.
- If help stays back: finish with controlled two-foot/one-foot gather and a high, soft release; protect the ball away from the defender.
- If the lane is closed: stop under control for a balanced pull-up or move the ball. Avoid jumping without a decision.
- Against size/strength, do not try to win every collision. Create an angle, use legal body positioning, finish away from shot blocker, or pass out.
- Contact drills must be controlled and supervised; do not seek dangerous collisions or initiate illegal contact.

### 3. Shooting choices
- Take open catch-and-shoot 3s in rhythm when feet and balance are ready.
- Do not force contested 3s to reach 15 points. Current 3PT practice sample is small (18/60 provisionally) and not a game percentage.
- Attack hard closeouts; if defender flies by, use one decisive dribble into paint or balanced pull-up.
- Use midrange pull-up only when the defender/screen/help gives that space and balance is good.
- Seek paint touches and free throws through good decisions, not foul hunting.

### 4. Rebounding and transition defense
- On defense: make first contact/box out before chasing the ball; locate Maciaszek and other crashers.
- On offense: after a shot, don't watch; either crash from a suitable lane or get back according to coach's rules.
- After a turnover or missed shot, sprint back and protect the rim first; communicate matchups.

## Scoring target: process, not quota
15 points could come from several combinations, e.g. 4 two-point field goals + 2 threes + 1 free throw = 15. This is an illustration, not a required shot distribution. A better target is to generate high-quality opportunities:
- open rhythm 3s when available;
- strong drives after a closeout or a defensive gap;
- balanced pull-ups when the paint is protected;
- extra points from transition decisions and free throws when naturally earned.
A good possession can be a pass that creates a teammate's open shot. Do not chase 15 at the cost of turnovers or team offense.
